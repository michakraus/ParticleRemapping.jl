Numerical experiments ​

The standard experiments with the kick–drift map (:projection) and the non-separable map (:nonseparable) of standard_method. The comparison sets the results beside those of the other constructions. The structural checks of the chapter are on the page structural statements and target weights after a push, the hole of the chapter's own example on the page hole filling.

The checkerboard ​

With the represented density, both maps stop before the fit: the target points of the density-interpolation flow cover the whole box, and the target weights vanish at the targets in and , where the represented density is zero. The construction needs a density that is positive on its box.

julia
using ParticleRemapping, CairoMakie
set_theme!(remap_theme())
represented = standard_checkerboard(:projection; variant = :represented)
represented.error
"DomainError with 0.0:\nthe target weights must be positive; a lumped projection on hats finer than the marker spacing vanishes at some targets"

With the positive density , both maps fill the empty quadrants:

The exact weight in   is . The total weight and the total momentum are kept to round-off. The markers have equal weights, so the stage Hamiltonians do not depend on the weight of a marker, and each map is the same area-preserving map for every marker (one map for all markers). It cannot shrink the area of the empty region, but it can fold the occupied region into filaments that reach into every quadrant, which fills cells but leaves empty cells between the filaments. The target weights are those of the positive density, so every marker keeps the weight .

julia
positive = standard_checkerboard(:projection; variant = :positive)

    empty = positive.stats_after.empty, report = positive.report)

plot_remap(positive.before, positive.after;

The kick–drift map leaves no cell empty. Its target weights come from the density at the targets, and where the targets reach the tails of the density the weights are small; a small weight at a fixed momentum gives a large velocity, up to . The relative difference of the smoothed densities, , is that of the positive density, whose tails the markers did not sample before. The non-separable map with its five fit iterations and    splines reaches its targets less closely and moves some markers to velocities up to .

Remaps during the run ​

With a remap after every 100 steps, the kick–drift map fails at the third remap ( ). The first remap leaves some markers outside the velocity box   of the construction. For these markers the density-interpolation flow sends the targets out of the box: the largest target speed is at  , at   and   at  , where the density underflows to zero and the target weights vanish. The construction assumes that every marker lies in its box; a repeated remap needs a box that grows with the velocities, or a limit on the velocities that the gauge step gives (limitations). The non-separable map fails with the same error. standard_remap_history reports the failure:

julia
h = standard_remap_history(:projection; variant = :positive)
h.error   # "DomainError with 0.0: the target weights must be positive; …"

The run takes about two minutes, so the manual does not repeat it.
