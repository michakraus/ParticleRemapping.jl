Limitations ​

  • The step reaches its targets only on the identity-connected branch. The parameters make a root of the step equations, but the forward Newton solve follows the branch from ; when the contraction condition fails by much, it ends on another root (the single symplectic Euler step of the experiments) or not at all (K3). Two close markers separated by in one step force   (remark (a)); substeps reduce the displacement of each step.

  • The relaxation does not scale with . The repulsion is a sum over all pairs, so the force on a point grows with the number of neighbours within the kernel widths, and a fixed step moves many points out of the velocity box (K2). The kernel widths match the spacing of the 16-marker lattice.

  • The targets must lie in the spline box. The projection, the number density and the KKT weights live on   ; no basis function is nonzero at a target outside it, and the KKT solve fails (K2).

  • The weights are positive only when  . The KKT correction keeps the discrete moments exactly, and its metric protects small coefficients, but positivity holds only when the quadrature discrepancy of the moments is small enough (KKT projection (iii)).

  • Symplectic in the open-loop sense only. The parameters are computed from the state; target matching in with weights tied to the density contracts an open set of states towards a lower-dimensional set of layouts, which no closed-loop symplectic map does (cotangent lifts). The variational closure is symplectic as a closed loop, but it spreads clusters by a repulsion and does not match targets.

  • Targets in , not in . Weights tied to the density fold the velocity–momentum map (fold); the construction therefore evaluates and forward from , and the coarse-basis regime recovers velocities along the branch of the fold (remark (b)).

  • Only the weight and the momentum are constrained. The kinetic energy, the grid charge and the grid current change.

  • The pairwise basis grows with the square of the momentum basis.   ; the pair sums cost , and the Newton solve works with the dense   Hessian (higher dimensions).

Open questions ​

  • The coarse-basis regime at large . With    the regularised parameter solve reaches the targets only approximately, and the velocities are recovered along the branch of the fold (remark (b)). The package tests the recovery on 16 markers; it has no experiment of this regime at large .

  • A relaxation that scales. Kernel widths that follow the spacing of an equidistribution, a step size that bounds the largest displacement, and a bound on the displacement of one step that keeps the contraction condition: the remark (a) gives the direction,   for a pair at distance , but no a priori bound of the contraction number by the displacements.

  • A variational closure that matches targets. The variational closure makes the closed loop symplectic; whether a potential exists whose stationary points move the markers towards the relaxed targets is open.

  • More moments in the KKT projection. The kinetic energy at the targets, , and the grid charge   are linear in the spline coefficients , like the mass and the momentum, so they can enter as further rows with the same KKT formula. Open are the rank of the larger and the positivity of under more constraints; the kinetic energy of the result equals the constrained value only when the step reaches the targets.

  • The collective form in the solver. The Fourier-moment Hamiltonian costs per evaluation (symmetric Hamiltonians (iii)); the package evaluates it, but the parameter solve and the Newton step use the pairwise spline form only.

  • Repeated remaps. The long-time behaviour of a simulation that redistributes with state-dependent parameters (open questions of the comparison).
