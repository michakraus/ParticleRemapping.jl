Constructions that fail ​

Each section names a construction that fails, the reason it fails, and the control that shows it. The controls marked port run in the package (test/sliced/experiments.jl, test/sliced/experiments_long_time.jl); the observations marked derivation come from the probes of the derivation of the sliced map and have no reproduction in the package.

Maps that do not couple the markers ​

A symplectic map applied to each marker on its own keeps every hole with its volume (uncoupled maps). This holds for any single-marker map, however it is built.

Lifts of the marginals ​

A quantile lift of all markers in one slice couples them, but in the limit of many markers it acts on each marker's own coordinate. Port, two controls:

  • checkerboard_experiment: one slice in each lift leaves the quadrant counts at , because both marginals of the checkerboard are already uniform. The conjugate variables stretch by up to   (largest 6.77).

  • rotated_frame_lift_probe: a symplectic lift of the derivation. A convex quantile lift of all 400 markers ( , mollifier , no target weights) in six rotated frames           per pass, four passes. It moves two markers into the empty quadrants: , the counts that the derivation reports.

The slices remove this: within a slice of small velocity range the positions are spread over the whole interval whatever their correlation with the velocity.

Reweighting at fixed momenta ​

After the lifts the markers sit at . Setting the weights from the density at that point and the velocity from   is symplectic (a gauge step), but the velocity grows where the weight is small, and the markers leave the region they were moved into.

  • Derivation: on the checkerboard, whose density is a step function, a kernel estimate of the input density vanished in the empty quadrants; the weights there went to zero and the velocities diverged (that probe also had an error in the units of the density ratio).

  • Port, checkerboard_reweighting_control: the sliced checkerboard reweighted by the kernel_density_ratio of the input (width 0.25, periodic on  ). The weights range over 0.171–2.35 times the input weight, and the largest speed is 5.69, against 1.04 after the lifts.

  • Derivation: after a pilot pass that left no cell empty, a kernel reweighting at fixed momenta emptied cells again. Port, the long-time run with squeeze = false (long_time_remap_experiment): 3 empty cells, relative standard deviation 0.740 (0.598 before the remap, 0.367 with the squeeze), difference 0.337, largest 31.9.

This is why the weight squeeze exists: it scales the momenta by the same factor as the weights, so the velocity of a slot stays where the lifts put it.

Multipliers that depend on the state ​

A squeeze whose multipliers are computed from the squeezed state is not the linear cotangent lift of a constant . Port, suite 3: multipliers      give the symplecticity defect 10.8. The density-ratio multipliers of SlicedQuantileRemap depend on the state too; that remap is canonical for frozen multipliers only (parameters that depend on the state). A choice that follows the density and keeps the map symplectic is an open question.
