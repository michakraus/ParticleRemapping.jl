Background ​

This chapter sets out the problem that the whole manual addresses. The weighted markers of a particle-in-cell method cluster in phase space, and a redistribution that moves them back should respect the Hamiltonian structure of the marker system. The chapter states the requirements on such a redistribution and gives the road map of the constructions. The canonical structure itself is the subject of the next chapter, Canonical structure of weighted markers.

Consider one species of charged particles with mass and charge in a self-consistent or external electric field . The phase-space density solves the Vlasov equation

Clustering and holes ​

The characteristic flow preserves phase-space volume. In the continuum limit a uniform marker density therefore stays uniform. For a finite number of markers this is not true at the resolution scale. Phase mixing stretches the initial arrangement into filaments, the local marker spacing becomes anisotropic, and regions appear that contain few or no markers. Such regions are called holes. A hole increases the noise of every moment that the markers deposit on a grid, and it leaves a part of phase space unresolved. With importance sampling, a hole in is also a hole in the marker distribution.

using ParticleRemapping, CairoMakie

f(x, v) = (1 + 0.3cos(x)) * exp(-v^2 / 2) / sqrt(2π)
E(x) = -0.5sin(x)

n = 40
x0 = vec([(i - 0.5) * 2π / n for i in 1:n, j in 1:n])
v0 = vec([-4 + (j - 0.5) * 8 / n for i in 1:n, j in 1:n])
w0 = f.(x0, v0) .* (2π * 8 / n^2)

function push(x, v, T; dt = 0.01)
    x, v = copy(x), copy(v)
    for _ in 1:round(Int, T / dt)
        v .+= dt / 2 .* E.(x)
        x .= mod.(x .+ dt .* v, 2π)
        v .+= dt / 2 .* E.(x)
    end
    x, v
end

empty_cells(x, v) = 16^2 - length(unique(zip(floor.(Int, x ./ (2π / 16)),
    floor.(Int, (v .+ 4) ./ (8 / 16)))))

fig = Figure(size = (1000, 330))
for (k, T) in enumerate((0.0, 10.0, 40.0))
    x, v = push(x0, v0, T)
    ax = Axis(fig[1, k]; xlabel = "x", ylabel = "v", limits = (0, 2π, -4, 4),
        title = "t = $(T), empty cells: $(empty_cells(x, v))")
    plot_markers!(ax, Markers(x, v, w0); colorrange = extrema(w0))
end

Remapping ​

A redistribution (remapping, resampling) step moves the markers back towards a regular arrangement and assigns new weights so that the represented density does not change. In its usual form it deposits the markers on a grid and samples new ones from the grid density. This is a coarse-graining step: it adds numerical diffusion and it does not respect the Hamiltonian structure of the marker system. This manual asks which redistributions of weighted markers are compatible with the canonical structure, and constructs four of them.

  • (R4) Equidistribution. The markers are approximately equidistributed afterwards.

Each construction meets (R1) exactly. Which of (R2)–(R4) it meets exactly, which approximately, and which further quantities (grid charge, kinetic energy, total weight) it keeps, is stated in its chapter and collected in the comparison.

The constructions ​

The weights add freedom. A change of weight at a fixed canonical momentum,  , is the identity on , so it is symplectic for any  , even one that depends on the state of the whole ensemble. The factorisation lemma of the next chapter shows that every symplectic redistribution is a canonical map of followed by such a gauge step. The chapter after it, Symplectic redistribution, shows what this leaves possible: a smooth canonical map applied to every marker moves a hole but cannot shrink it (Liouville), so a redistribution that fills holes either changes the weights, or moves the markers with a map that depends on their weights or couples them.

The first two chapters move the markers to target points with a map whose parameters are fitted or solved for. The third keeps the canonical map fixed, the identity or a given map, and chooses the new weights. The fourth builds an explicit map that couples the markers. Every chapter runs the same standard experiments. Where the parameters, slices or multipliers are computed from the markers, the map is symplectic for their frozen values; Parameters that depend on the state states what this means and when it can be closed. Each construction implements the common interface remap(method, markers), which returns a RemapResult with the new markers and the diagnostics of the method. conservation_report compares two marker sets.

The constructions come from four sets of research notes: Hamiltonian remapping of weighted particles in 1D-1V, Hamiltonian particle projection: a symplectic redistribution of weighted markers, Symplectic redistribution of weighted particles in phase space, and a derivation of the sliced redistribution, whose note is written from the sliced chapter. The verification chapter lists which number of each source the package reproduces.
