Limitations ​

Gauge rezoning.

  • Rays. A marker moves only along its velocity ray. It cannot cross  , and it does not move in . A hole that lies across  , or a hole in , needs a canonical map in addition. A per-marker momentum translation    is canonical for fixed ; it keeps the grid current if   for every node, and the kinetic-energy constraint then includes the translation.

  • The fold. The homotopy stops where some curvature reaches zero: no nearby minimiser continues the branch (second-order condition, one constraint in closed form). The clustered example stops at  , and each further constraint lowers the reached: with   (experiments).

  • Representation of . The weight follows from the move:  . The construction does not ensure that is close to times the phase-space area of the marker; the moments outside change. Targets taken from an estimate of , for example those of the current-quantile matching, combine the two aims (combining the two constructions).

  • Targets. The default targets keep the order and the range of the speeds of each group of cell and sign; a group of fewer than three markers keeps its speeds. With few markers a single constraint need not keep the order of the speeds (one constraint in closed form).

  • Local energy constraints. The package constrains the kinetic energy at every node. The single global constraint   suffices for exact energy conservation and leaves more freedom; the package does not implement it.

Canonical transport.

  • Compatibility. Consistent weights exist only if preserves the sign-resolved current profile (compatibility); after an inadmissible lift the compatibility residual reaches and the representation of fails (experiments).

  • All freedom is in . For a given the weights are unique, and for   they are the old ones (uniqueness).

  • No equidistribution. A smooth canonical map with consistent weights cannot make the marker distribution uniform, and it cannot fill a region in which the canonical marker density is small; it can only move it (the limit).

  • One sheet. The matching treats one layer of the velocity–momentum sheet (the fold); a marker with   lies on no branch, and current_quantile_match rejects it.

  • The quantile rule. The total weight, the kinetic energy and the grid charge are reproduced only to the accuracy of the quantile rule, which decreases with the number of markers per cell and branch (five markers in one cell, example). The slowest marker of a branch gets the largest change of weight, since both its and its are small (example).

  • The target density. The package implements the cumulative current of a separable density   only (SeparableMaxwellianTarget); an estimate of from the markers before the remap fits the algorithm but is not implemented.

Open questions ​

  • Admissible generators. Which admissible generators reduce clustering most, and how much such a remap gains within the limit of equidistribution.

  • Folded sheets. How the matching splits between the layers of a folded sheet (the fold).

  • Several velocity dimensions. The matching along rays holds in the continuum (higher dimensions); a discrete matching with direction bins, its error in the angular resolution, and the admissible canonical maps in dimensions are open.

  • Repeated remaps. The long-time behaviour of a simulation with repeated remaps, given that a state-dependent remap breaks the symplecticity of the composite map (two readings).

  • The solver in 3d3v. The number of conjugate-gradient iterations, or the fill-in of a sparse factorisation, of the Schur complement on nodes; the package measures neither.

  • A larger reach. Whether the global energy constraint, or targets from an estimate of , move the fold of the homotopy to a larger .

  • A dynamical test. Holes that a simulation produces, rather than the clustered and sampled sets of the examples.

Obstacles and a route to 3d3v ​

  • The Schur solve. The dense factorisation of the package (K9) does not reach 3d3v. The Schur complement is a sparse, symmetric, positive definite matrix with a -node stencil in blocks of size , so a sparse factorisation or the conjugate-gradient method applies; how many iterations the latter needs is open.

  • Markers per group. The targets need at least three markers per group of cell and direction. With  ,   and the   orthants as directions, a group holds markers on average.

  • Rays in three dimensions. The gauge step cannot redistribute in angle; a hole in the direction of needs a canonical map, as a hole across   does in 1D.

  • The fold. The argument is the same in every dimension, and every additional constrained moment lowers the reached in 1D (experiments).

  • A discrete matching along rays. The continuum identity holds; the discrete algorithm needs direction bins, the radial cumulative current of averaged over each bin, and canonical maps that keep the direction-resolved current. None of the three is implemented, and the admissible generators in dimensions are not derived.

A route to 3d3v: tensor-product hat functions on nodes, the scalar stretch per marker and the diagonal Hessian as in 1D, and a sparse solve of the Schur complement for the gauge rezoning; for the transport, cotangent lifts with , direction bins, and the radial cumulative current of the target density.
