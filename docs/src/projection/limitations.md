## [Properties](@id projection-properties)

| quantity | status |
|:--|:--|
| symplectic form ``\omega`` | exact for every fixed ``\theta``; ``\theta`` comes from a fit to the state ([parameters that depend on the state](@ref state-dependent-parameters), [structural statements](@ref projection-checks)) |
| total weight | exact, by the normalisation ``c`` of the [target weights](@ref projection-targets); the charge-conserving weights keep it by the partition of unity ([charge-conserving reweighting](@ref projection-charge)); [example](@ref projection-example) |
| total momentum ``P`` | exact: the centroid shift of the [kicks](@ref projection-hamiltonian) and of the [non-separable stages](@ref projection-nonseparable), and the [gauge step](@ref gauge-step); [example](@ref projection-example), [hole filling](@ref projection-hole-filling) |
| kinetic energy | not kept: the gauge step changes it from ``\sum_a \lvert p_a\rvert^2 / (2 m w_a)`` to ``\sum_a \lvert p_a\rvert^2 / (2 m w^*_a)``, and the energy constraint of [the fit](@ref projection-fit) is not implemented; [example](@ref projection-example) |
| grid charge | not kept with the target weights; exact on the periodic cubic B-splines of the [charge-conserving reweighting](@ref projection-charge) ([hole filling](@ref projection-hole-filling), [push](@ref projection-push-test)) |
| grid current | not kept: the map moves positions and momenta, and the charge-conserving reweighting leaves the momenta as the map made them ([charge-conserving reweighting](@ref projection-charge)); [example](@ref projection-example) |
| represented density (consistency) | up to the fit residual and the projection error of ``f_h`` (consistency, below) |
| equidistribution and hole filling | up to the fit residual and the resolution of the hats of the density-interpolation flow; at the hole of the experiment where ``\partial_x W = 0``, the fit of the kicks and drifts from ``\theta = 0`` ends on a mirror-symmetric saddle that leaves the hole open, and they fill it once the fit breaks the symmetry; the non-separable stages fill it from ``\theta = 0`` ([hole filling](@ref projection-hole-filling)) |
| parameters computed from the state | the target points (density-interpolation flow), the target weights, the parameters ``\theta`` (least-squares fit) and the multipliers ``\lambda`` of the charge-conserving reweighting |
| cost of one remap | linear in ``N`` for the projection, the flow, the map and the reweighting; the fit costs ``O(N n_\theta^2 + n_\theta^3)`` per Gauss–Newton iteration with ``n_\theta`` parameters, the QR factorisation of its ``(2dN + n_\theta) \times n_\theta`` Jacobian, and the Poisson problem of the flow ``O(M^3)`` on its ``M`` nodes ([the cost in 3d3v](@ref projection-cost-3d3v)) |
| dimensions implemented in the package | the stages (kicks, drifts, non-separable stages) in any ``d``, tested for ``d = 1, 2``; the whole algorithm for ``d = 1`` ([what the package implements](@ref projection-implemented)) |

**Consistency.** The new weight is ``w'_a = c f_h(y^*_a)``, while the new marker is at
``y'_a = (x'_a, v'_a)``. The residual of the consistency condition (R3) is

```math
w'_a - c f_h(y'_a) = c \big( f_h(y^*_a) - f_h(y'_a) \big)
= O\big( c \, |\nabla f_h| \, |y'_a - y^*_a| \big) ,
```

with ``v'_a - v^*_a = (p^S_a - p^*_a) / (m w^*_a)``. It is bounded by the fit residual. The
projection error of ``f_h`` adds to it.

**Equidistribution.** The new markers are equidistributed up to the fit residual and up to the
resolution of the spline basis in the density-interpolation flow. By the [compressibility
bracket](@ref weight-dependent-hamiltonians), the fit can reduce the residual only where the old
weights vary. In regions where the old weights are uniform, and in the whole domain if all weights
are equal, the fit residual cannot vanish. For the separable stages, the [stage
Jacobians](@ref projection-stage-jacobians) restrict the compression further; the [non-separable
stages](@ref projection-nonseparable) do not have this restriction.

**Near the identity.** At ``\theta = 0`` the map is the identity. For small ``\theta`` it is a
near-identity symplectic map generated by a smooth Hamiltonian in ``(x, p, w)``, with as many
parameters as the spline basis has functions, independent of ``N``.

## [Limitations](@id projection-limitations)

- **No compression where the weights are uniform.** A stage Hamiltonian changes the marker density
  only at the rate of the [compressibility bracket](@ref weight-dependent-hamiltonians)
  ``\{W, \partial_w \Psi\}``. Markers of equal weight, and markers loaded proportionally to ``f``,
  are moved by one area-preserving map, and their holes persist; only the gauge step changes their
  velocities.
- **The kicks and drifts couple ``+v`` and ``-v``.** Where ``\partial_x W = 0`` and ``W`` is even
  in ``v``, a kick that compresses the markers at ``+v`` expands them at ``-v``
  ([stage Jacobians](@ref projection-stage-jacobians)). At ``x_h = \pi``, a point with
  ``\partial_x W = 0``, this coupling makes the fit of the hole-filling experiment from
  ``\theta = 0`` lead to a mirror-symmetric saddle of its objective, where the hole stays open
  (fill ``0.47``). Once a seeded start breaks the symmetry, the kicks and drifts fill the hole
  (fill ``0.97`` to ``1.06`` after the 40 iterations, not at a minimum); the non-separable
  stages fill it from ``\theta = 0`` (``0.93`` to ``0.94``)
  ([hole filling](@ref projection-hole-filling)).
- **The map is symplectic for frozen parameters only.** The targets, ``\theta`` and the target
  weights come from the state, so the remap is not one symplectic map of the state
  ([parameters that depend on the state](@ref state-dependent-parameters)).
- **The non-separable stages end at the solvability of the midpoint equation.** The fit rejects
  every ``\theta`` whose fixed-point iteration needs more than 50 iterations, and the fitted
  ``\theta`` of both hole-filling runs sits at that limit. One application costs 0.20 to 0.34 s
  against 0.004 to 0.007 s for the kicks and drifts in the run of the script
  ([hole filling](@ref projection-hole-filling)).
- **The target weights must be positive and accurate in the tails.** The lumped projection
  vanishes between the markers on hats finer than the marker spacing, and `remap` then throws a
  `DomainError`. Its relative error in the tails of a Maxwellian reaches a root mean square of
  ``0.21`` in ``|v| \in [3, 4)``, and the reweighting passes it on to the velocities
  ([reweighting](@ref projection-final-reweighting)). The low-rank density bounds an absolute
  error, so its relative error grows with ``|v|`` too ([push](@ref projection-push-test)).
- **The charge-conserving reweighting needs markers under every charged shape function.** If a
  shape function with ``\rho_i > 0`` has no marker after the map, no weights reproduce the grid
  charge, and `remap` reports it. It keeps the charge deficit of a hole and lowers the fill of the
  hole from between ``0.93`` and ``0.94`` to ``0.70``
  ([hole filling](@ref projection-hole-filling)).
- **Neither the kinetic energy nor the grid current is kept.** The fit has no energy constraint,
  and the reweighting acts on the weights only.
- **The density-interpolation flow equidistributes at the resolution of its hats.** On hats
  coarser than the markers the targets keep part of a hole, as in the [example](@ref projection-example).
- **1D-1V only.** `remap` with [`KickDriftRemap`](@ref), [`NonSeparableRemap`](@ref) or
  [`ChargeConservingReweighting`](@ref) throws an `ArgumentError` for markers in more dimensions.

## [Known issues](@id projection-known-issues)

The entries of `KNOWN_ISSUES.md` of the package that concern this chapter:

- **K10** — the density-interpolation flow solves its Poisson problem with the pseudo-inverse of a
  dense matrix on the nodes of the hats, ``O(M^3)`` operations on ``M`` nodes, which limits the
  code to ``d = 1`` ([the cost in 3d3v](@ref projection-cost-3d3v)).

## [Open questions](@id projection-open-questions)

- **The solver of the non-separable stages.** The fit ends at the iteration limit of the
  fixed-point solver, so the solvability of the midpoint equation limits the step; a Newton solver,
  more stages with smaller steps, or a composition of kicks, drifts and non-separable stages may
  relax this.
- **A parametrisation for ``d = 3``.** The tensor product of the non-separable stages has
  ``n_\xi^d n_v^d n_\ell`` parameters per stage, ``2.4 \cdot 10^8`` for two stages in 3d3v with the
  bases of the experiment; a sparse or low-rank parametrisation is needed there.
- **A fit for many parameters.** The Gauss–Newton fit forms and factors the dense Jacobian of all
  parameters; whether the adjoint gradient with L-BFGS or a matrix-free Gauss–Newton method reaches
  the same objective is untested.
- **The projection of the weights.** A dynamical low-rank density at ``\vartheta \leq 10^{-5}``
  gives a small relative error in the tails in the [test after a push](@ref projection-push-test),
  at rank 13 or less, in a weak, fixed field in ``d = 1``. Open are the rank that a
  self-consistent field and ``d = 3`` need, a truncation that bounds the relative error in the
  tails, and the sign of ``f_r``, which the test never saw negative.
- **A dynamical test.** The holes of the experiments are cut out by hand, and the push only shears
  them. A test on holes that a simulation produces, for example in a bump-on-tail instability, is
  open.
- **Charge against filling.** The charge-conserving reweighting keeps the grid charge of the
  markers before the map, with the charge deficit of a hole. Which charge is the right reference
  depends on whether the hole is a defect of the sampling or a feature of ``f``.
- **The energy.** The fit can take the kinetic-energy constraint with a Lagrange multiplier; how
  much it costs in the objective is not measured.
- **The target density.** An equidistribution is one choice. A target marker density
  ``g \propto f_h^a`` with ``0 < a < 1`` moves the fold outwards, to
  ``|v| = v_{\mathrm{th}} / \sqrt{1 - a}`` for a Maxwellian, and reduces the spread of the weights.
- **Composition with the push.** The composition of the push and the map is symplectic as a
  sequence of maps for fixed weights and fixed ``\theta``, not as one map of the initial state
  ([two readings](@ref two-readings)). Whether the push conserves ``P`` depends on the field
  discretisation.

## [Higher dimensions](@id projection-higher-dimensions)

### Statements in d dimensions

The structural facts hold in ``d`` dimensions: the [compressibility
bracket](@ref weight-dependent-hamiltonians), the [fold](@ref velocity-momentum-fold) of the
implicit weight and [one map for all markers](@ref one-map-for-all-markers); the tests check the
first two for ``d = 1, 2, 3``. The [kick–drift map](@ref projection-hamiltonian) is symplectic and
conserves ``P`` in ``d`` dimensions; the centroid ``X_{\mathrm{c}}`` and the boost are ``d``
numbers. The [non-separable stages](@ref projection-nonseparable) keep all four items of their
proposition, and the markers couple only through the ``2d`` numbers ``c`` and ``b``. The stage
Jacobians generalise by the matrix determinant lemma: a kick ``v \mapsto v + \Delta v(x, \ell)``
with ``\ell = \ln W(x, v)`` has the Jacobian ``I + \partial_\ell \Delta v \otimes \nabla_v \ell`` in
``v``, so

```math
\det DK = 1 + \nabla_v \ln W \cdot \partial_\ell \Delta v , \qquad
\det DD = 1 + \nabla_x \ln W \cdot \partial_\ell \Delta x ,
```

and the coupling of ``+v`` and ``-v`` holds wherever ``\nabla_x W = 0`` and ``W`` is even in
``v``. The projection, the density-interpolation flow, the target weights, the reweighting and the
charge-conserving reweighting are defined on ``\Omega = \mathbb{T}^d \times [-V, V]^d`` without
change; the [proposition](@ref projection-charge) on the charge-conserving weights holds for shape
functions on ``\mathbb{T}^d``. The low-rank density factors into functions of ``x \in \mathbb{T}^d``
and of ``v \in \mathbb{R}^d``.

### [Implementation in the package](@id projection-implemented)

The stages act on ``d \times N`` arrays: [`centroid_kick`](@ref), [`velocity_drift`](@ref) and
[`nonseparable_stage`](@ref), with their symplecticity, ``P`` and the non-separable Jacobian
tested for ``d = 1`` and ``d = 2`` (`test/projection/stages.jl`). The compressibility bracket and
the fold are tested for ``d = 1, 2, 3`` (`test/projection/structure.jl`). Everything else is
``d = 1``: [`HatProjection`](@ref) and [`moser_laplacian`](@ref) on ``[0, L) \times [-V, V]``, the
parametrisations [`KickDriftParametrisation`](@ref) and [`NonSeparableParametrisation`](@ref),
[`fit_remap_parameters`](@ref), the charge splines, the BUG integrator and the diagnostics of the
experiments (an elliptic hole in ``(x, v)``).

### [The cost in 3d3v](@id projection-cost-3d3v)

Let ``n_\xi``, ``n_v``, ``n_w`` (or ``n_\ell``) be the numbers of cubic splines per direction,
``n_X``, ``n_V`` the numbers of hats per direction, ``n_g`` the number of charge shape functions
per direction, ``S`` the number of stages and ``n_{\mathrm{it}}`` the fixed-point iterations of a
non-separable stage. A cubic B-spline basis has 4 nonzero functions at a point in each direction,
a hat basis 2. The table gives the sizes with the bases of the
[hole-filling experiment](@ref projection-hole-filling) in every direction
(`scripts/projection/higher_dimensions.jl`, which checks the column ``d = 1`` against the code).

| quantity | formula | ``d = 1`` | ``d = 3`` |
|:--|:--|--:|--:|
| kick–drift parameters, ``S = 2`` | ``S (n_\xi^d + n_v^d) n_w`` | 2304 | 3096576 |
| non-separable parameters, ``S = 2`` | ``S n_\xi^d n_v^d n_\ell`` | 3264 | 241483776 |
| nodes of ``f_h``, ``g_h`` and ``\phi`` | ``M = (n_X n_V)^d`` | 1056 | 1177583616 |
| charge multipliers | ``n_g^d`` | 32 | 32768 |
| hats at one marker | ``2^{2d}`` | 4 | 64 |
| spline terms of one kick at one marker | ``4^{d+1}`` | 16 | 256 |
| spline terms of one midpoint iteration at one marker | ``4^{2d+1}`` | 64 | 16384 |
| nonzeros of a row of the charge Hessian | ``7^d`` | 7 | 343 |

The steps of one remap cost:

1. **Projection.** ``O(N 2^{2d})`` for the deposit of ``f_h`` and ``g_h``; each field on the
   nodes of the hats takes 9.4 GB in 3d3v.
2. **Poisson problem of the flow.** The code forms the dense ``M \times M`` Laplacian and its
   pseudo-inverse, ``O(M^3)`` operations; in 3d3v the matrix alone has ``1.4 \cdot 10^{18}``
   entries. The Laplacian is a sum of second differences in each direction, periodic in ``x`` and
   with Neumann conditions in ``v``, so a Fourier transform in ``x`` and a cosine transform in
   ``v`` diagonalise it, at ``O(M \log M)``.
3. **Flow.** ``O(n_{\mathrm{steps}} N 2^{2d} d)``: four Runge–Kutta stages per step, each with
   the interpolation of ``g_h`` and of the ``2d`` components of ``\nabla \phi`` at every marker.
4. **Map.** ``O(S N d \, 4^{d+1})`` for the kicks and drifts; ``O(S N n_{\mathrm{it}} d \,
   4^{2d+1})`` for the non-separable stages, plus a ``2d \times 2d`` solve per marker and
   iteration.
5. **Fit.** The Gauss–Newton method forms the Jacobian of the ``2dN`` residuals and the
   ``n_\theta`` regularisation rows with respect to the ``n_\theta`` parameters and factors it by
   QR, ``O(N d \, n_\theta^2 + n_\theta^3)`` per iteration; it forms no normal matrix. In 3d3v the
   regularisation rows of the Jacobian alone have ``9.6 \cdot 10^{12}`` entries for the kicks and
   drifts and ``5.8 \cdot 10^{16}`` for the non-separable stages.
6. **Charge-conserving reweighting.** ``O(N 4^{2d})`` per Newton iteration for the assembly of the
   Hessian, with ``7^d`` nonzeros per row, and a sparse solve of ``n_g^d`` unknowns.
7. **Low-rank target weights.** The full grid of the [test after a push](@ref projection-push-test)
   (64 points in ``x``, 256 in ``v``) has ``4.4 \cdot 10^{12}`` points in 3d3v; the low-rank
   factors store ``r (n_x^d + n_v^d) + r^2`` values, ``1.7 \cdot 10^7`` per unit of rank. The
   evaluation at the targets costs ``O(N r)`` interpolations of the factors.

### Obstacles and a route to 3d3v

- **The 2d-dimensional grid.** The hat projection and the density-interpolation flow live on a
  grid in ``2d`` dimensions: ``1.2 \cdot 10^9`` nodes with the hats of the experiment. A
  transform-based Poisson solver removes the cubic cost of step 2, not the storage. A low-rank
  representation of ``g_h`` would store ``O(r (n_X^d + n_V^d))`` values; the Laplacian is a
  Kronecker sum, but its inverse is not of low rank, so a solver in such a format needs an
  approximation of the inverse, which is open.
- **The parameters.** The tensor products give ``3.1 \cdot 10^6`` parameters for the kicks and
  drifts and ``2.4 \cdot 10^8`` for the non-separable stages in 3d3v. A sparse or low-rank
  parametrisation of ``\psi_s`` is open.
- **The fit.** The dense Jacobian does not fit into memory in 3d3v. The adjoint recursion of
  [the fit](@ref projection-fit) gives the gradient of the objective at the cost of one sweep
  forward and one backward through the stages, ``O(S N d \, 4^{d+1})`` for the kicks and drifts,
  independent of ``n_\theta``; with it, L-BFGS or a matrix-free Gauss–Newton method (Jacobian
  products by one forward and one adjoint sweep) replaces the dense Jacobian.
- **The target weights.** The lumped projection on the ``2d``-dimensional grid shares the storage
  of the first item. The [low-rank density](@ref projection-lowrank) is the route of this chapter
  for ``d = 3``: its storage grows with ``n_x^d + n_v^d`` instead of their product, and the target
  weights need only its values at the targets.
- **The diagnostics.** The empty disk, the fill and the elliptic hole are defined in ``(x, v)``
  for ``d = 1``; a test in 3d3v needs their analogues in six dimensions.
