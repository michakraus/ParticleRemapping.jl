Hamiltonian particle projection ​

By the factorisation lemma, every symplectic redistribution is a canonical map of followed by a gauge step. In this chapter, is the map of a parametrised Hamiltonian that depends on the weights of the markers: a composition of exactly integrable kicks and drifts, or of non-separable stages that the implicit midpoint rule integrates. A least-squares fit chooses the parameters so that the markers reach the target points of a density-interpolation flow. The gauge step then gives the markers their new weights at fixed : the values of a projected density at the target points, the minimiser of a relative entropy under the constraint of the grid charge, or the values of a dynamical low-rank density. For every fixed , the map preserves exactly, and it conserves the total momentum and the total weight exactly. The charge-conserving variant also keeps the grid charge. Consistency (R3) and equidistribution (R4) hold up to the fit residual and the resolution of the projection (properties).

The chapter Symplectic redistribution states four facts that shape the construction:

  • The gauge step. A change of weight at fixed is the identity on (gauge step). The construction puts all its symplectic content into the motion of , and it assigns the new weights in a final gauge step, the reweighting.

  • One map for all markers. A Hamiltonian that does not depend on the weights moves all markers with one volume-preserving map (one map for all markers), so holes persist. The stage Hamiltonians of this chapter therefore depend on the weights, through splines in .

  • Weight-dependent Hamiltonians. Such a Hamiltonian compresses the marker density at the rate of the compressibility bracket, that is only where the old weights vary. The stage Jacobians refine this in : where  , a kick that compresses the markers at expands them at . The non-separable stages do not have this coupling.

  • The velocity–momentum fold. The implicit equation   for a new weight at fixed has two solutions below a bound and none above it (the velocity–momentum fold). The construction never solves this equation: it takes the new weight from the target point and computes the velocity from it.

The fit makes a function of the state, so the map is symplectic for each fixed , in the sense of parameters that depend on the state. The spline-projection redistribution also moves the markers to target points; there the Hamiltonian couples the markers instead of depending on their weights.

Example ​

The markers sit on a   lattice in   , with the weights   for   , and an elliptic hole with the semi-axes and around removes some of them (hole_filling_problem). The kick–drift map with two stages remaps them (KickDriftRemap): the targets come from the density-interpolation flow on   hat functions, the target weights are at the targets, so that the example shows the map and not the error of a projection, and the fit ends after its budget of 20 iterations, with the return code MAXITERS. The result reports the number of markers, the objective after the fit relative to  , the return code of the fit and the relative changes of the total momentum and the total weight. The same example gives the figures projection-remap.pdf, projection-stages.pdf and the animation projection-stages.mp4 of scripts/projection/make_figures.jl.

julia
using ParticleRemapping, CairoMakie, Random
Random.seed!(20261119)
small = hole_filling_problem(; hole_x = π / 2, nL = 24, hole_a = 0.9, hole_b = 0.8)
method = KickDriftRemap(; projection = HatProjection(12, 13), maxit = 20,
    density = (xs, vs) -> small.density.(xs, vs))
result = remap(method, small.markers)
(; N = small.N, objective_ratio = result.info.objective_ratio, code = result.info.status.code,
    momentum_change = result.info.momentum_change, weight_change = result.info.weight_change)
(N = 550, objective_ratio = 0.32824304591433145, code = GeometricSolvers.MAXITERS, momentum_change = 2.425127150268611e-17, weight_change = 0.0)

The code ​

The module ParticleRemapping.Projection implements every construction of this chapter for   (and the stages in any dimension). Three method types implement remap, remap_states and, for the first two, remap_frames:

  • KickDriftRemap: the whole algorithm with the kick–drift map;

  • NonSeparableRemap: the whole algorithm with the non-separable implicit-midpoint stages;

  • ChargeConservingReweighting: the charge-conserving choice of the final weights.

The experiments are functions with the parameters of this chapter as defaults: hole_filling_experiment and dlra_weights_experiment; experiment_remap gives the states and the frames of each map of the hole-filling experiment. The scripts scripts/projection/hole_filling.jl, scripts/projection/dlra_weights.jl and scripts/projection/checks.jl print every number of the structural statements and of the hole filling, scripts/projection/higher_dimensions.jl prints the sizes of the higher dimensions, and scripts/projection/make_figures.jl writes the figures and animations to results/projection/. The verification lists every test file and script of the chapter.
