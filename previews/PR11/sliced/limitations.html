Properties, limitations and higher dimensions ​

This page collects what the sliced redistribution keeps, what it cannot do, its known issues and open questions, and what it needs in dimensions, in particular in 3d3v.

Limitations ​

  • Canonical only for frozen parameters. The slices, their orders and anchors and the multipliers come from the state. The map is canonical for each frozen choice (the composed map); as a map of the state it is not, by the closed-loop identity (K5). Multipliers that depend on the squeezed state break the canonical form of the squeeze by in suite 3 (multipliers that depend on the state).

  • The squeeze trades velocity for position. keeps the velocity of a slot at the weight and scales the offset   by . Slots with small multipliers, in the tails of the density, move their markers far from their anchors: up to 3.46 in suite 5b (K6). Skipping the squeeze moves the problem into the velocities, up to 31.9 (reweighting at fixed momenta).

  • Relaxation, not target matching. The lifts relax towards the targets with  . The Hessian is bounded below by  , and the cotangent part amplifies the conjugate variables by up to its inverse;   reaches the targets and makes the lift stiff (stiff relaxation and narrow mollifiers).

  • A box and a slicing. The targets are uniform in the box    , the slices have equal numbers of markers, and must be a multiple of and . The weights carry the density; the marker distribution is pulled towards uniform in the box, whatever the density.

  • Only what the slices see. A lift of one slice sees the marginal of its coordinate and cannot fill a hole with uniform marginals (lifts of the marginals). In   the per-axis map slices each pair and cannot fill a hole in the correlation of two axes (higher dimensions).

  • No grid moments and no energy. The weights and the velocities keep the total weight and the total momentum; nothing in the construction constrains the kinetic energy or the grid charge and current, which change in the standard example.

  • Dense linear algebra. Each lift assembles and solves a dense   system (K7).

Known issues ​

The entries of KNOWN_ISSUES.md of the package that concern this chapter; the notes of the particle-in-cell project list none for it.

  • K5 — the long-time sliced remap is canonical only for frozen multipliers: remap computes from the state by a predictor–corrector loop.

  • K6 — the weight squeeze can move markers far from their anchors, by up to 3.46 in the long-time experiment of 900 markers.

  • K7 — each sliced lift solves a dense   system with an LU factorisation, , although the Hessian is block diagonal plus a rank-one term.

Open questions ​

  • Multipliers. A choice of the multipliers that follows the density and keeps the map symplectic as a map of the state, for example from a generating function (multipliers that depend on the state).

  • Slices that depend on the state. Whether the map with slices, orders and anchors read from the state is injective across the sets where two markers swap their order (the sliced quantile lift).

  • Many markers in one slice. The mean-field limit of a one-slice quantile lift, and the rate at which slices of finite size escape the obstruction of uncoupled maps (coupling is necessary, not sufficient).

  • Correlations between axes. Which lifts fill a hole in the correlation of two axes with fewer slices than the full conditioning of the cost in 3d3v: lifts conditioned on a few chosen coordinates, or lifts in rotated frames (see obstacles).

  • Repeated remaps. The remaps during the run cover   with four remaps; the long-time behaviour with many remaps, and the effect of the far moves of K6 on the dynamics, are not tested.

Implementation in the package ​

For   markers, remap with a SlicedQuantileRemap applies and to each pair: velocity slices by for the positions , position slices by for the momenta (one SlicedLiftPlan per axis). It computes one set of multipliers from the product kernel in all coordinates and squeezes every axis with them (per-axis version). test/sliced/peraxis.jl and suite 3 check it in 2D-2V.

Each lift reads its slices and targets from the two-dimensional marginal only, so the lifts cannot see a hole in the joint distribution of different axes, such as in , or . The analogue of the checkerboard one dimension up shows it: 360 equal-weight markers in 2D-2V fill the quadrants and of the plane, with velocities uniform on   and independent of the positions, so every marginal is even. The checkerboard of the same markers in is the control:

using Random: MersenneTwister

N, h = 360, 180
method = SlicedQuantileRemap(; B_v = 6, B_x = 36)

r = remap(method, Markers(x, v, fill(1 / N, N)))

v1 = vcat(rand(rng, h), -rand(rng, h))
r1 = remap(SlicedQuantileRemap(; B_v = 6, B_x = 36, Vmax = 1.0),
    Markers(x1, v1, fill(1 / N, N)))
x̄ = periodic_wrap.(r.markers.x, π)
(; x1_v1_before = phase_quadrant_counts(x[1, :], v[1, :]),

Obstacles and a route to 3d3v ​

  • The dense solves (K7). The block-diagonal plus rank-one solve makes each lift ; it changes no statement of the chapter, because the Hessian and the map are the same.

  • The density ratio. Its pair sums dominate once the lifts are linear in . With the default widths, a kernel holds 5.0 markers of a uniform cover at   in 1D-1V, but   in 3d3v, and the same five markers need    (the numbers above): the widths, or the estimator, must change with the dimension.

  • Correlations between axes. The per-axis map sees two-dimensional marginals. A full conditioning needs slices in its last lift, which is out of reach for any useful in 3d3v. Between the two lie lifts conditioned on a few chosen coordinates, and lifts in rotated frames: an orthogonal rotation of the positions with the same rotation of the momenta,  , is canonical, and a per-axis lift in the rotated frame sees other two-dimensional marginals. Which of them fill the holes of a kinetic simulation is an open question.

  • The state dependence and the far moves (K5, K6) remain in every dimension.
