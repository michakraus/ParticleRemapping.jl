Numerical experiments ​

The standard experiments with the gauge rezoning (:gauge) and the canonical transport with the identity map (:transport) of standard_method. The comparison sets the results beside those of the other constructions. The two numerical examples of the chapter are on the page clustered markers and a separable density.

Both constructions keep every position: the gauge rezoning by construction, the transport because its canonical map is the identity. Both keep every momentum and every sign of . They can spread the velocities of the markers in a cell, but they cannot move a marker into another cell of or across  .

The empty quadrants stay empty for both densities. In a cell with   every marker has  , so the transport matches only the branch   of the target density there, whatever weight the target puts on the other branch. The gauge rezoning keeps the total weight, the total momentum and the kinetic energy to round-off, and the grid charge by its constraint. The transport keeps every momentum; its weights follow the quantiles of the target, and the total weight changes by the quantile error of 50 markers per cell and branch.

julia
using ParticleRemapping, CairoMakie
set_theme!(remap_theme())
gauge = standard_checkerboard(:gauge)
transport = standard_checkerboard(:transport; variant = :positive)
(gauge = (gauge.quadrants_after, gauge.report),
    transport = (transport.quadrants_after, transport.report))
(gauge = ((200, 0, 200, 0), (weight = 0.0, momentum = 3.469446951953614e-18, kinetic_energy = 0.0)), transport = ((200, 0, 200, 0), (weight = 0.015767667092079662, momentum = 6.938893903907228e-18, kinetic_energy = 0.0018776157602422017)))
julia
plot_remap(gauge.after, transport.after;
    titles = ("(a) after the gauge rezoning", "(b) after the transport, positive density"))

The gauge rezoning spreads the speeds in each cell and sign towards an even spacing, as far as the homotopy reaches under the constraints of the grid charge and the kinetic energy; the number of empty cells falls from 14 to 13. The transport with the represented density gives each marker the velocity at its quantile of the exact density of the run; the result stays close to the markers before. With the positive density the quantiles reach into the tails of , which the markers never sampled, so the velocities spread over the whole box and every cell holds a marker. The closure of the quantiles then puts the weight of the unsampled tails nowhere: the total weight falls by .

julia
long_gauge = standard_long_time(:gauge)
long_transport = standard_long_time(:transport; variant = :positive)
(gauge = long_gauge.stats_after[(:empty, :rstd)], gauge_l2 = long_gauge.l2_difference,
    transport = long_transport.stats_after[(:empty, :rstd)],
    transport_weight = long_transport.report.weight)
(gauge = (empty = 13, rstd = 0.5585195356101593), gauge_l2 = 0.06940374125685644, transport = (empty = 0, rstd = 0.39152247989767347), transport_weight = -0.20134308881949592)
julia
plot_remap(long_gauge.after, long_transport.after; period = (-π, π),
    titles = ("(a) after the gauge rezoning", "(b) after the transport, positive density"))
