The verification set-up ​

The defaults of SplineRedistribution fit the verification set-up of 16 markers: there every step of the construction does what the chapter states, and the symplectic step reaches its targets to round-off. This page runs that example step by step, compares the schemes and the substeps on it, and then shows a case outside it, where the relaxation fails (known issues K2). The page hole filling shows the case where the symplectic step fails (K3). The script scripts/redistribution/failure_cases.jl prints every number of this page (Julia 1.13.4, about a minute).

The standard example in detail ​

redistribution_example starts from   markers at the cell centres of a lattice and clusters them by 60 leapfrog steps in a static force:

julia
using ParticleRemapping, CairoMakie
set_theme!(remap_theme())
ex = redistribution_example()
mk = ex.markers
plot_remap(ex.initial, mk; period = (0, ex.Lx),
    titles = ("(a) lattice", "(b) after 60 leapfrog steps"))

The leapfrog steps keep the weights. One remap with the defaults:

julia
method = SplineRedistribution()
res = remap(method, mk)
i = res.info

Every run keeps the total momentum to round-off (at most  ). The single symplectic Euler step converges to another root of its equations, a caustic of the remark (a); the substeps keep each contraction number below one and reach the targets. Its stages show where the single step ends:

julia
se = SplineRedistribution(; scheme = SymplecticEulerStep())
rse = remap(se, mk)
plot_stages(remap_states(se, mk, rse); targets = (rse.info.targets_x, rse.info.targets_v),
    period = (0, ex.Lx))

Panel (c) shows the end point of the step at the old weights; in (d) some markers sit away from their targets (open circles) after the gauge step.

Outside the verification set-up ​

More markers: the relaxation leaves the velocity box (K2) ​

The relaxation sums the repulsion over all pairs, and its step size fits 16 markers. With 900 markers of uniform positions, standard normal velocities and equal weights, the same ten steps move the points by up to and push of the targets out of the velocity box   of the spline basis, up to  :

julia
using Random
rng = MersenneTwister(1)
x9, v9 = 2π .* rand(rng, 900), randn(rng, 900)
r9 = declustering_relaxation(x9, v9; Lx = method.Lx, σx = method.σx, σv = method.σv)
fig = plot_target_relaxation(r9.history; Lx = method.Lx)
hlines!(content(fig[1, 1]), [-method.Vmax, method.Vmax]; color = :gray25, linestyle = :dash)
fig

The dashed lines bound the velocity box. remap(SplineRedistribution(), markers) on these markers stops in the KKT solve with ArgumentError: matrix contains Infs or NaNs.

Summary ​

On the verification set-up, each step of the construction holds: the relaxation reduces the clustering, the KKT weights are positive and keep the discrete moments, and the symplectic step reaches the targets, in one step for the midpoint rule and in two substeps for symplectic Euler. The contraction condition is sufficient, not necessary: the midpoint step reaches the targets with the contraction number , and the single symplectic Euler step with does not. Outside the set-up, the relaxation parameters do not scale with the number of markers, and a single step to distant targets meets no root on the identity-connected branch. The limitations state the reasons.
