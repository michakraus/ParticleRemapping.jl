Spline-projection redistribution ​

In this chapter the canonical map is one symplectic step of an ensemble Hamiltonian that couples the markers in pairs. The Hamiltonian is a linear combination of spline functions of the relative positions   and of the momenta. It is invariant under translations and permutations of the markers, so the step keeps the total momentum . Its parameters solve a linear system, so that the step moves the markers onto relaxed, approximately equidistributed targets . The new weights come from a positive spline projection of the distribution, corrected by Karush–Kuhn–Tucker (KKT) conditions so that the total weight and the total momentum are exact. By the factorisation lemma, the step followed by this gauge step keeps exactly for fixed parameters, and it keeps and the total weight exactly.

A canonical map applied to each marker on its own moves a hole but does not shrink it (uncoupled maps). The pairwise Hamiltonian is free of this obstruction because it couples the markers. The new weights depend on the projected density at the targets, so the velocity–momentum fold rules out a choice of followed by a solve for : the chapter specifies the targets in and evaluates and   forward. The parameters are computed from the markers, so the map is symplectic for their frozen values, in the open-loop reading of two readings of symplecticity; the closed-loop identity states what a closed loop needs. The Hamiltonian particle projection also fits the parameters of a Hamiltonian to target points; here the Hamiltonian does not depend on the weights, and the parameters solve the linear system of one step. The sliced redistribution also couples the markers, by an explicit map without parameters.

The algorithm step by step ​

The figures follow the steps on the 16 markers of the standard example below (algorithm_svg). The markers are coloured and sized by weight; open circles are the relaxed targets.

  1. Project. Project the markers onto a tensor-product B-spline basis, periodic in and clamped in , by the positive lumped projection (panel 1, the dashed lines are the knots).

  2. Relax. A few damped steps of repulsive gradient descent move the points to targets (panel 2, arrows from the markers to their targets).

  3. Weigh. The weighted KKT projection gives the target weights  , which keep the discrete mass and momentum (panel 3 draws the targets with these weights).

  4. Step. One linear solve gives the parameters of a pairwise spline Hamiltonian whose symplectic step moves to ; at the old weights the end point lies on the targets in but not in (panel 4).

  5. Gauge step. The end point gets the weights at fixed (panel 5).

Example ​

The standard example is the verification set-up redistribution_example:   markers at the cell centres of a lattice on   , weighted by   with   , mass  , and clustered by 60 leapfrog steps of length in the static force   . SplineRedistribution with its defaults redistributes them by one implicit midpoint step. The figure shows the markers before and after the remap, coloured and sized by weight, with the relaxed targets as open circles:

julia
using ParticleRemapping, CairoMakie
set_theme!(remap_theme())
ex = redistribution_example()
method = SplineRedistribution()
res = remap(method, ex.markers)
targets = (res.info.targets_x, res.info.targets_v)
plot_remap(ex.markers, res.markers; targets = targets, period = (0, ex.Lx),
    titles = ("(a) before: clustered markers", "(b) after: redistributed markers"))

The total weight and the total momentum are kept to round-off. The kinetic energy is not a constraint of the construction and changes by of (scripts/redistribution/failure_cases.jl). The pages of this chapter ​

  • Positive, moment-compatible spline projection: the projection keeps mass and momentum but overshoots to negative values in sparse regions; the de-clustering relaxation gives the targets; the weighted KKT projection gives positive weights with the exact discrete mass and momentum that the parameter solver needs.

  • Symmetric ensemble Hamiltonians: uncoupled Hamiltonians cannot exchange momentum while they keep the total momentum, and the subtraction of the mean force breaks symplecticity; the pairwise, Fourier-moment and centroid-frame Hamiltonians are invariant under permutations and translations.

  • Symplectic step and parameter solver: for  , one step of the implicit midpoint or the symplectic Euler method gives a linear system for on the  -dimensional subspace of zero net momentum; the forward Newton solve reaches the identity-connected branch when  ; caustics, substeps and the coarse basis.

  • Closed loops: the closed-loop identity evaluated on the parameter maps of this chapter, the fixed-grid rule, the closed-loop cotangent lifts and the two closed loops of the verification.

  • Algorithm: the five steps of SplineRedistribution, from the positive projection to the gauge step.

  • Numerical experiments: the standard experiments, which every construction runs.

  • The verification set-up: the standard example in detail, the schemes and substeps, and the relaxation outside the verification set-up (K2).

  • Hole filling: on a marker set with a hole, the symplectic step misses its targets and remap does not report it (K3).

  • Properties, limitations and higher dimensions: what the map keeps, what it cannot do, the known issues, the open questions, and the cost in 3d3v.

  • Library: Redistribution: the functions of the module.

The code ​

The module ParticleRemapping.Redistribution implements the construction for  , with positions on and velocities in   by default. The method type SplineRedistribution implements remap, remap_states and remap_frames; its keywords set the spline basis, the relaxation, the Hamiltonian basis, the scheme and the substeps. The building blocks are public: the projections (l2_spline_projection, kkt_spline_projection), the relaxation (declustering_relaxation), the Hamiltonians (PairwiseSplineHamiltonian, FourierMomentHamiltonian, CentroidFrameHamiltonian), the parameter solve and the step (solve_redistribution_parameters, forward_redistribution_step) and the closed loops (closed_loop_defect).

The script scripts/redistribution/verify_symplectic_redistribution.jl checks every statement of the note and prints its numbers; scripts/redistribution/failure_cases.jl prints the numbers of the verification set-up, of hole filling and of the limitations; scripts/redistribution/make_figures.jl writes the figures and the animations to results/redistribution/. The verification lists every test file and script of the chapter.
