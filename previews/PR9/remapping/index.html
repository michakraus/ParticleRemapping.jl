Gauge rezoning and canonical transport ​

By the factorisation lemma, every symplectic redistribution is a canonical map of , symplectic for   , followed by a gauge step. This chapter works out the two extreme cases of this factorisation in 1D-1V, on a periodic grid with nodes and linear shape functions . Both are exactly symplectic for .

  • The gauge rezoning takes  . It moves no marker in : it stretches each velocity along its ray,  , and divides the weight by the same  . A constrained optimisation chooses the stretches. The positions, the momenta, the total momentum and the grid current are exact by construction, and the grid charge and the kinetic energy are exact by constraints. The gauge step preserves also for stretches that depend on the whole ensemble, so the remap itself needs no closed-loop argument (parameters that depend on the state); only the composition with the flow is subject to the two readings.

  • The canonical transport takes a given canonical map , the same for all markers, and chooses the new weights by current-quantile matching, a gauge step at the mapped , so that the markers represent the same density . In the continuum limit these weights are unique, and they exist if and only if preserves the sign-resolved current profile. One map for all markers moves holes but keeps their area; the limit of equidistribution states this obstruction for consistent weights.

Gauge rezoning ​

The standard example of the gauge rezoning is gauge_rezoning_setup:   markers on   nodes of , whose velocities cluster around six random centres in each cell, with weights from a two-stream density, and the constraints  . The figure shows the markers before and after the remap, coloured and sized by weight. The positions and the momenta are the same in both panels; the targets are not drawn, since an open circle for each of the 6400 markers would hide them.

julia
using ParticleRemapping, CairoMakie
set_theme!(remap_theme())
gauge = gauge_rezoning_setup()
mk, grid = gauge.markers, gauge.grid
r = remap(gauge.method, mk)
plot_remap(mk, r.markers;
    titles = ("(a) before", "(b) after, τ = $(round(r.info.τ; digits = 3))"))

The canonical map is the identity, so the states of the remap are points of the homotopy: the markers before, the gauge steps of two accepted homotopy steps, and the result at the parameter where the homotopy stops (remap_states). Every state has the same ; only the velocities and the weights change.

julia
plot_stages(remap_states(gauge.method, mk, r); columns = 2, panel_size = (400, 280))

The animation follows the homotopy through its accepted steps, with the log-stretches interpolated between them (remap_frames):

julia
animate_stages("remapping-gauge-stages.mp4", remap_frames(gauge.method, mk, r))

The changes of the conserved quantities (conservation_report); the grid moments are relative to the moments of :

The code ​

The module ParticleRemapping.Remapping implements both constructions in 1D-1V on a PeriodicGrid. The method types GaugeRezoning and CurrentQuantileMatching implement remap, remap_states and remap_frames. The steps of the gauge rezoning are public: the targets (gauge_targets), the constraints and the optimality conditions (gauge_constraints, gauge_stationarity, gauge_hessian), the Newton step (gauge_newton_step) and the homotopy (gauge_homotopy). So are those of the transport: the cotangent lift (cotangent_lift) and the matching of one branch and of a grid (branch_quantile_matching, current_quantile_match).

The tests in test/remapping/ check every claim and every number of the chapter, and the scripts in scripts/remapping/ print the numbers of its tables; scripts/remapping/make_figures.jl writes the figures and the animations to results/remapping/. The verification lists what is checked where.
